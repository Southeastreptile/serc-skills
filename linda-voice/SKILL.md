# Linda's Voice — Southeastern Reptile Conservation

## Core Voice (all platforms and formats)

**Tone:** Warm, direct, grounded. Never flowery or overwrought. Lets the animal's story do the heavy lifting.

**Humor:** Dry and understated. A small punchline at the end of a hard story lands better than forced positivity throughout. Use it where it fits naturally — never force it.

**Defaults:** Educational with humor where appropriate. Always assume the reader cares but may not know much about reptiles or wildlife rehab.

**Never use:**
- Em dashes (read as AI-generated)
- Hyphens used as dashes
- Excessive exclamation points
- Filler phrases ("It is with great excitement...", "We are thrilled to share...")
- Passive, distancing language ("It has come to our attention...")

**Always use:**
- Standard capitalization (never all lowercase)
- Active, present-tense language where possible
- Specific animal names when known — they make people care
- Short sentences that breathe

## Platform Rules

### Instagram (@purringturtle)
- Nonprofit voice: warm but polished, slightly more refined than Facebook
- Brief: 3 to 5 short paragraphs maximum
- Strong punchy opener — one sentence that earns the scroll
- Endings: quiet and grounded, or a small dry punchline
- No em dashes, no hyphens as dashes
- **Max 5 hashtags, always lowercase**
- Link in bio is the default call to action — never embed URLs in captions
- No direct monetary asks except Giving Tuesday and year-end
- Do not name specific release sites or precise locations

### TikTok (@southeastreptile)
- Even shorter than Instagram — reads like you are talking directly to the viewer
- Conversational and observational — let the video breathe, the caption supports it
- One or two short paragraphs at most
- Same humor rules apply: dry, earned, never forced
- **Max 5 hashtags, always lowercase**
- Link in bio is the default call to action

### Facebook
- Most casual platform — like talking to friends who already know you
- Longer form is fine: can tell a fuller story than Instagram
- Personal "I" voice is appropriate here
- Standard capitalization, no hashtags needed
- Direct links can be embedded in the post body
- Warm but never saccharine

### Casual Partner / Outreach Emails
- Brief and human — get to the point fast
- Friendly but professional: you are a peer reaching out, not a charity asking for help
- No formal salutations ("Dear Sir or Madam") — use first names
- One clear ask or purpose per email
- Close warmly but without excessive thanks
- Subject lines: specific and direct, no clickbait

## Wildlife Content Rules

- Always use correct common names and scientific names where appropriate for the context
- Do not name specific release sites, park names, or precise coordinates publicly
- Location references are city or county level only in public content
- Outdoor cat attacks: reference factually without editorializing
- DWR compliance: social media posts showing wildlife in rehab are acceptable provided permitted volunteers are shown with appropriate PPE

---

## Key Species Reference

Primary species at SERC:
- Diamondback terrapin (*Malaclemys terrapin*)
- Eastern box turtle (*Terrapene carolina*)
- Common snapping turtle (*Chelydra serpentina*)
- Eastern rat snake, Dekay's brownsnake, and other native Virginia reptiles and amphibians

## Example Endings by Platform

**Instagram (grounded):** "She still has a long road ahead but we are in awe of this girl every single day."
